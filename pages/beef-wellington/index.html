Eye Round Beef, And Only A Little Coconut Oil

We choose eye round beef on purpose — it is one of the leanest beef cuts, with less fat than fattier cuts. That is the start of our less-oil standard, not a cheaper fatty cut.

Coconut oil is the fat for the pastry layers, and we use it in a small, controlled amount only. Human-grade, plant-based, just enough for a golden pastry — not a heavily-oiled pastry. Eye round + a little coconut oil is why it eats rich, but not greasy.

Why Choose Rabbit?

Does Your Dog Refuse Mushrooms?

First, the basics: dogs can eat cooked, store-bought mushrooms — cremini, button, portobello and similar culinary varieties. Wild mushrooms are a different story and must never be used, and raw mushrooms are not ideal either, because their tough cell wall is hard for dogs to digest.

So what is actually in a cremini mushroom? Naturally, B vitamins including riboflavin and niacin, plus selenium, potassium, zinc and copper, and antioxidant compounds like ergothioneine, which is not destroyed by cooking. It is also low in calories, with no fat or cholesterol, and very little salt — so it adds savoury depth and moisture to the filling without adding a lot of fat.

But many dogs dislike raw mushroom's earthy, musty smell. So we don't put raw mushroom in. We finely chop cremini mushroom and cook it down into a duxelles, with an extra cooking step specifically to remove that smell before it goes into the filling.

And the duxelles is plain mushroom only — no onion, no garlic, no salt, no heavy seasoning. Cooked and plain is the part that makes it suitable for dogs in the first place.

Reduced To The Minimum: Just 6

We cut it down to the minimum — 6 ingredients, and no more. Nothing extra, nothing just for show.

Eye round beef for the meat, cremini mushroom for the filling, egg to bind it, oat flour for the pastry, a small amount of coconut oil for the pastry fat, and goat milk powder in the filling. Take one away, and it is not the same Wellington.

The full list is on the card attached to every box, so you can count all 6 yourself. No wheat flour, no hidden ingredient.

We Will Launch Rabbit As Option For Sensitive Dogs

We know rabbit is hard to find and hard to reach, so we want to make rabbit available for all dogs. Now sensitive dogs can enjoy their treats like everyone else.

  • Plenty Of Protein, Little Fat

    Rabbit is packed with the protein dogs need to thrive, and it is a naturally lean meat: low in calories and cholesterol, which helps prevent obesity.

  • Promotes Muscle Development

    The amino acids in rabbit protein, such as isoleucine and valine, help repair tissue and support muscles.

  • Essential Vitamins And Coat Health

    Rabbit is a source of vitamins such as B12, which boosts blood cell growth and helps digestion, and its healthy fats keep a dog's coat sleek and skin healthy.

  • A Novel Protein Source

    Rabbit is less common in commercial dog food, and is often considered for dogs with sensitivities to common proteins.
